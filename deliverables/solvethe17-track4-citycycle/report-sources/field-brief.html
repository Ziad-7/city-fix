CityCycle: Field Deployment Brief

Turn sorted waste into economic value while strengthening existing collectors. CityCycle connects households, informal collectors and district staff through pickup requests, recorded weights/cash amounts and smart-bin information.

Current proof of concept: software and a PC simulator; Wokwi source for an ESP32 bin and hub scale. No physical unit or field trial exists. The field solar/GSM circuit is a paper design. This brief describes how deployment would work, not measured field performance.

1. Operate with local partners

Start in one neighbourhood with participating collectors, a collection hub, a receiving recycler and the district. Co-design the route, price display and dispute process with workers. Use existing bins, scales and phones where practical; place new sensors only where overflow information changes collection decisions.

Households request a pickup; collectors accept, weigh materials, record cash paid and complete the job; households confirm. Staff inspect requests and alerts. Recycler receipts and collector net-income tracking are proposed next steps, not current verified app features. No electronic payment processing is claimed.

3. Access, worker benefit and privacy

Use Egyptian Arabic, English, familiar material icons, large controls and assisted onboarding. Test comprehension with collectors and households instead of assuming literacy or smartphone access. Existing mobile queues support offline work; validate reconnect behaviour on the pilot phones. Non-smartphone access is a future channel, not an implemented guarantee.

Publish dated material prices and explain estimates versus final weights. Manual entries must remain distinguishable from future authenticated scale readings. Agree a weight/payment dispute process and participation terms without displacing collectors. Restrict home locations and financial records by role; keep keys private and obtain consent before photo analysis. Device telemetry has no camera.

5. Cost and viable operation

The accompanying CityFix-BOM-Estimate.csv and COST-ESTIMATE.md record sourced prices and quantities, separating hardware from the connectivity reference. Priced bin components total 7,243.00 EGP; the custom mount still needs a workshop quote. This is not a complete installed cost. Labour, vehicles, installation, existing bins/phones/computers, taxes/delivery and paid cloud/provider usage need separate quotations.

Do not promise payback until hardware, maintenance and connectivity costs can be compared with observed trip savings or service value. Confirm who funds operations with the district/partner; no funding agreement or revenue result is claimed.

6. Pilot gates and evaluation

  1. Software demonstration: run pickup → collector → household confirmation and simulated reading → API → staff history. Test lost connection and retries; label demo prices and data.
  2. Bench gate: finish field firmware, safe power design, per-bin calibration and scale integration. Measure accuracy, currents and outage recovery; complete heat, dust and splash checks.
  3. Neighbourhood pilot: agree sites, collector participation, recycler acceptance and a baseline. Proposed planning scope: 2–4 weeks; partner agreement and resources are not yet secured.
  4. Evaluate before expansion: measure collection time, overflow duration, contamination and cost/kg. Recycler-confirmed recovery and collector net earnings require receipt/cost tracking; current collected kg and material value are not substitutes. Agree numerical targets after baseline measurement.

Pilot and future features are proposals. Exact rollout size, hardware prices, solar sizing, service thresholds and success targets require partner agreement and measured evidence. Prepared for submission; not a field-validation certificate.
